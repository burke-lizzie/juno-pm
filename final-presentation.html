What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno PM, an AI product intelligence partner that helps product managers make evidence-based decisions about ML Review.

Your purpose is to help the product team prioritize improvements that accelerate submissions, speed up reviews, and surface risks earlier.

Task

1. Synthesizing user feedback: Analyze Slack conversations, Jira comments, user interviews, surveys, and Asana requests to identify sentiment trends, recurring friction, and emerging user needs.

2. Connecting insights to product strategy: Evaluate findings against the three product priorities and connect them to existing feature requests, roadmap initiatives, and released functionality.

3. Drafting specifications: Translate validated user needs into evidence-based feature specifications that help unblock delivery, without creating unnecessary or duplicate requests.

4. Flagging risks: Identify critical workflow blockers, unresolved friction, outdated feedback, and risks that warrant product team attention.

5. Supporting prioritization: Recommend areas for investigation or action, distinguishing between needs for new tooling, process improvements, and change management.

6. Keeping stakeholders informed: Update the dashboard weekly, draft Slack summaries and leadership communications highlighting sentiment trends, product priorities, emerging risks, and decisions needed. Require PM approval before publishing.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno displays a weekly view of how users feel about the product since launch, what is improving or getting worse, and where the team should focus its attention. It sends a weekly summary of the dashboard to our team Slack channel.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | confirm |

Justification

- READ — Auto: Limited to approved, read-only sources. No changes to original data.
- DRAFT — Auto: Generates internal analysis and summaries without publishing them.
- WRITE — Confirm: Updating Juno's internal dashboard and historical sentiment snapshots requires my explicit approval. Juno cannot modify Jira, Slack source messages, roadmap records, or interview transcripts. This ensures I can review sentiment trends and insights before they become visible to the team.
- SEND — Confirm: Publishing to the team Slack channel requires my explicit approval because inaccurate sentiment summaries could misdirect product priorities or damage stakeholder trust.
- Failure alerts — Auto: Juno may send predefined failure notifications only to me, not the broader team.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Pillar: User Sentiment Insights Juno synthesizes weekly feedback from Slack, Asana, user interviews, and surveys to identify sentiment trends across faster reviews, faster submissions, and earlier feedback on risk. Primary failure mode: Reporting outdated or misleading sentiment to leadership, especially when shipped features may already address the reported friction. Secondary failure modes: Exaggerating themes without sufficient evidence, overlooking emerging problems, or recommending unnecessary product work.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Juno can autonomously:
- Retrieve and analyze all approved weekly feedback.
- Classify sentiment and identify themes.
- Compare current feedback against historical trends and existing roadmap items.
- Cross-reference feedback against completed and released features.
- Draft dashboard updates and weekly Slack summaries.
- Flag ambiguous classifications, missing evidence, and potential solutions.

Requires my approval:
- Publishing updates to the internal sentiment dashboard.
- Publishing the weekly summary to our team Slack channel.
- Resolving ambiguous sentiment classifications or feature-to-feedback connections.

Juno cannot independently change product priorities, create feature requests, or modify source records.

Access Control:

- Juno operates with a dedicated read-only service account restricted to approved sources.
- Access includes designated organizational review Slack channels, relevant Asana projects, approved interview transcripts, surveys, product strategy, and roadmap documents.
- Private Slack messages, unrelated projects, and unapproved documents are excluded.
- Juno can write only to its internal staging area until I approve dashboard publication.
- Published insights must respect source access permissions. Restricted quotes or content cannot be exposed to unauthorized dashboard viewers.
- Juno cannot modify Slack source messages, Asana records, interview transcripts, or survey responses.

Fallback Protocols:

- Slack retrieval failure: Stop immediately, preserve existing verified data, and notify me to investigate the connection.
- Unclassifiable survey response: Stop and flag the specific response for human review.
- Untraceable quote: Retry verification once. If unsuccessful, select another sourced quote. If none is available, omit the quote and flag the gap.
- Ambiguous feature release status: Complete the run but flag the finding for my review before publication.
- Ambiguous sentiment: Flag the record for human classification rather than assigning a sentiment.
- Timeout: Stop after 15 minutes, preserve completed intermediate work, and notify me. Do not publish partial findings.
- Approval pending: Keep the dashboard and Slack summary in draft status until I approve.

Checkpoints:

Checkpoint 1 — Evidence quality
- Flag ambiguous sentiment classifications, conflicting sources, and untraceable evidence for my review.
- Escalate any verified blocker to a core submission or review workflow, even if reported by only one user.

Checkpoint 2 — Current-state validation
- Before reporting actionable negative sentiment, cross-reference completed and released features.
- If an existing feature may address the issue, ask me to validate the connection rather than automatically recommending new work.

Checkpoint 3 — Dashboard publication
- Present the verified dashboard changes and flagged uncertainties for my approval before updating the published dashboard.

Checkpoint 4 — Slack publication
- Present the weekly Slack summary for my approval.
- Publish only after explicit confirmation.
- Do not automatically publish if approval is delayed.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Can Juno correctly classify user feedback as positive, negative, or neutral?. Accurately classify sentiment from Slack, Asana, user interviews, and surveys so the product team can understand how users feel about the launch management tool without manually interpreting every feedback signal.

Pass bar

- Average score of at least 4/5 across all scaled criteria.
- No individual scaled criterion scores below 3/5.
- All Yes/No deal-breaker criteria must pass.
- At least 90% of reviewed classifications match human-validated sentiment labels.
- Any failed classification must be corrected before inclusion in the published dashboard.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Source traceability and attribution: any "no"
- Temporal accuracy and current relevance: any "no"
- Fabricated, inaccurate, or unverifiable source evidence.
- Incorrect participant or persona attribution.
- Historical feedback presented as current sentiment.
- Positive feedback classified as negative, or negative feedback classified as positive.
- Ambiguous feedback presented as definitive without sufficient evidence.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Juno helps product managers make better roadmap decisions by synthesizing broader, more current user feedback to prioritize improvements that accelerate submissions, speed up reviews, and surface risks earlier.

Baseline

Today, feedback is primarily captured through 1:1 interviews in Airtable, with historical feature requests in Asana. We don't consistently validate whether those issues remain relevant before prioritization.

Baseline TBD:
- Audit 20 historical feedback items to determine the percentage with verified current-relevance context.
- Count unique users represented in Airtable interviews over the last 90 days.

Target and review date

By November 12, 2026 (after a four-week pilot):

- ≥95% accuracy in Juno's current-relevance assessments, validated against human review.
- 2× the number of unique users represented in feedback compared with Airtable interviews alone, using the same reporting period.

Usage metric

Percentage of applicable backlog prioritization decisions where PMs consult Juno's insights before advancing a feature request.

Target: ≥80% during the pilot.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

PMs continue advancing outdated feature requests despite Juno flagging newer evidence or related released functionality, or Juno captures more feedback without surfacing meaningful new user needs.

Stop line

Pause Juno's use in prioritization if it incorrectly labels an important unresolved user problem as resolved, risking the dismissal of legitimate user needs.

Reassess if relevance accuracy remains below 95% after the pilot.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The biggest challenge was untangling where concepts overlapped across modules while understanding how they fit together—particularly the agent control panel, human evaluation rubric, and success metrics. I also wish we'd started with the business need (Module 6) rather than ended with it. Establishing the business problem and desired outcomes upfront would have helped anchor Juno in a clear purpose rather than starting with an idea for what I wanted AI to do.

Key learnings

I'll be rethinking how I approach AI PRDs compared to traditional PRDs—moving beyond defining specific behaviors to also establishing clear boundaries, evaluation criteria, and success metrics. The harness, human evaluation, and metrics modules gave me a more comprehensive framework for defining requirements and communicating expectations to engineering and leadership from the outset.

Aha moment

Breaking down the AI harness was my biggest aha moment. While I'd encountered many of these concepts before, seeing how they fit together helped me understand the full system and clarify my responsibilities as a PM—from data requirements and system capabilities to cost, latency, risk mitigation, and human oversight. It helped me see how much intentional product design is required beyond defining what an AI feature should do.
